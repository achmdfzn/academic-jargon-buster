## 1.27.0 — 2026-11-13

- New tool: Readability, the Flesch Reading Ease + Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level for a text (Flesch 1948, J. Appl. Psychol. 32(3), 221–233) — pure formulas on word/sentence/syllable counts, no language model, no API, no minimum word count. RE = 206.835 − 1.015 × (words/sentence) − 84.6 × (syllables/word), clamped to 0–100; FKGL = 0.39 × (words/sentence) + 11.8 × (syllables/word) − 15.59, clamped to a minimum of 0; both rounded to 1 decimal, with the standard Flesch bands (90+ Very easy, 80+ Easy, 70+ Fairly easy, 60+ Standard, 50+ Fairly difficult, 30+ Difficult, below 30 Very difficult). Syllables are counted by EYD rules (vowels a/e/i/o/u; diphthongs ai/au/oi only — "ei" is not one, so "survei" counts 3 and "keindahan" 4; a word with no vowel counts 1). `analyze` always computes it and shows it in the report, the `--json` output, and the web UI (new Readability card in the Analisis Kualitas tab, under the score gauge) — but it never changes the 0–100 score, the four score parts, or the exit code. New subcommand `readability <file|--text> [--lang id|en] [--json]` (informative: exit 0 always, except bad input) and thin shim `readability.py`; no new menu option. Every result carries the honesty note: the Flesch formulas were calibrated for English, so on Indonesian text the score is an indication, not a verdict — Indonesian academic prose usually lands lower because Indonesian words average more syllables, and that is expected, not a bug. Calibration (measured, not claimed): a stiff academic paragraph ("Penelitian ini menggunakan metode survei. Data dikumpulkan melalui kuesioner.") scores RE 0.0, FKGL 28.1 — "Sangat sulit"; a humanized version of the same content ("Saya memakai survei untuk penelitian ini. Saya sebarkan kuesioner untuk mengumpulkan data.") scores RE 0.0, FKGL 21.2 — still "Sangat sulit"; a casual narrative paragraph (96 words, the HUMAN_1 calibration text) scores RE 12.7, FKGL 14.1 — still "Sangat sulit". So on Indonesian text the standard Flesch bands compress at the bottom — even casual prose lands "Sangat sulit" — and the FKGL is what still separates texts (28.1 stiff, 21.2 humanized, 14.1 casual). 30 new tests; full suite 490/490. `READABILITY_VERSION` 1.0.0; `QUALITY_VERSION` 1.2.11 → 1.2.12; `CLI_VERSION` 1.17.1 → 1.18.0.

## 1.26.0 — 2026-11-12

- New tool: AI Detector, a heuristic check whether a text reads like AI writing — pure stdlib, no language model and no API. `detect_ai()` scores four signals 0–100 (burstiness, weight 30: coefficient of variation of sentence lengths, 100 at CV 0 and 0 at CV 0.55; template phrases, weight 30: density of a curated original list of 20 Indonesian + 10 English transition phrases per 100 words, 100 at 5 per 100; lexical diversity, weight 20: type-token ratio, 100 at TTR 0.42 and 0 at 0.72; starter repetition, weight 20: share of sentences sharing the modal opening two words, 100 at a 0.50 share and 0 at 0.20) and takes the weighted mean of the applicable signals only. Verdict bands: ≥ 70 likely AI, 40–69 mixed / uncertain, < 40 likely human; under 30 words the verdict is "too short" with `ai_score` None, and an empty text is the plain `teks kosong` error. Every sentence is scored too (most AI-like first: 60% template phrase, 30% closeness to the mean sentence length, 10% modal starter). The honesty note is always attached: the score is an indication, never proof — human text can be flagged and AI text can pass, so it must never be used to accuse anyone. Calibration: 6 original texts in `tests/test_detector.py` (3 human-like, 3 AI-like, written from scratch) — human-like score 0.0, AI-like 78.9 / 66.8 / 65.8, and a typical human methods paragraph scores 80.5 while a light-AI text scores 83.5 — both capped to "Campuran / tidak pasti" in v1.26.1 because only 2 of 4 signals apply to such short texts.
